# OctoAcme Project Management Docs

## Overview

OctoAcme manages cross-functional work through a clear lifecycle: initiation, planning, execution, release, and retrospective. The team starts by validating the business need, identifying stakeholders, and defining measurable success criteria before committing to delivery. Once approved, work is translated into a prioritized backlog with clear owners, acceptance criteria, and milestones so execution stays focused and predictable. During delivery, teams use a project board, standups, and regular reviews to track progress, address dependencies, and keep quality visible.

After a release or milestone, the team captures lessons learned, updates risks and communication plans, and tracks a small set of improvement actions. This approach emphasizes ownership, customer value, iterative delivery, and continuous improvement while keeping communication and quality practices consistent across projects.

## Process Summary

OctoAcme’s project management processes are designed to align stakeholder expectations with execution realities. Teams begin with project initiation, where they complete a one-pager, confirm the problem and goals, identify stakeholders, and decide whether to move forward with planning. Planning turns an approved initiative into a backlog, milestone map, and release plan, with estimates, acceptance criteria, and Definition of Done documented up front. Execution then focuses on small, testable increments, handoff between roles, and regular status tracking to manage risk and dependencies early.

The project model also relies on role clarity and strong communication. Product managers define outcomes and priorities, project managers coordinate schedules and communications, developers deliver the work, and QA validates quality and acceptance criteria. Teams share progress through standups, weekly status updates, and milestone or incident communication templates so stakeholders remain informed. Quality assurance is built into the workflow through unit and integration testing, CI validation, security scanning, smoke tests, and review before release.
